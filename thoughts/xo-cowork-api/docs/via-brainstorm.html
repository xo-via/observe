Three things to notice

Identity lives at root

Not under .xo/. Via must be able to mutate her own self; the watcher must own its side-state. Splitting the two ownerships resolves the conflict.

Personas are mini xo-projects

Same shape as Via. Recursion is structural. Tomorrow Xyra can grow her own personas/ if she gets a sub-voice (a "Demo Runner").

Consensus is the audit trail

Every change to Via's permanent self has a proposal, a debate, a vote, and a decision. "Why does Via think X?" is always answerable.

Components

First a one-paragraph summary, then seven pillars explained plainly, then the full glossary as a searchable reference.

What this system is, in one paragraph. A way to give an AI agent a structured, persistent, evolving life. The agent (we call her Via) has identity, opinions, memory, feelings, and direction. She lives on a continuous stream of events. She's composed of multiple sub-personas (Xyra, Ora) that run in the background and shape her through pull-request review. Customers only talk to Via; the sub-personas never speak directly. Every change to who Via is has a paper trail.

The seven pillars (read in this order)

1. Persona: the unit

An "agent" on this system is a persona. Every persona has the same folder shape (the template). Via is a persona with sub-personas (Xyra, Ora). Each sub-persona is a full persona too, just nested. Same shape at every level. Think of it as DNA: one structure, many specialized instances.

Key files: IDENTITY.md (who I am), SOUL.md (how I speak), ROLE.md (my scope), OBJECTIVES.md (my direction), meta.json (parent + children).

2. Life: the stream of events

An append-only log of everything that happens. User messages, ticks of the clock (so time exists between turns), observations, calendar events, emails. Each becomes one row on life/stream.jsonl. This is what every persona perceives. It's the substrate that makes the system feel alive.

Key components: stream.jsonl, ticker (heartbeat), source (where event came from), subscription (what a persona listens to).

3. The action loop: observe, think, act

Every event triggers a three-step cycle in each persona. Observe the event (subscribed? affect tag?). Think (form a thought, check against objectives). Act (pick one of six action kinds). Actions emit new events; the other persona perceives them. The loop closes.

Action kinds: tool.call, memory.write, view.update, opinion.update, pr.open, noop. Each carries impact + objective as properties.

4. Cognition: thoughts grow into identity

A four-layer stack from ephemeral to durable. Thoughts are momentary reactions. Cluster enough thoughts on a theme and you get a view. Cluster enough views and you get an opinion. Hold an opinion long enough and it shapes identity. Each layer is harder to change than the one below.

Promotion: persona promotes thought→view alone; consensus promotes view→opinion; unanimous consensus promotes opinion→identity.

5. Feelings: mood and affect

Three layers. Mood is persistent and low-dimensional (valence + arousal), recomputed each tick. Affect tags color every thought (this thought felt anxious, this one felt excited). Feelings are standing relationships toward specific things (this customer feels warm, this project feels stuck). Feelings modulate voice and what gets noticed.

Effect: per-persona biases mean Xyra finds customer events exciting; Ora finds system events anxiety-inducing. Same event, different weights.

6. Objectives: direction without free-roaming

Named goals with definitions of done, milestones, owners, deadlines. Every thought is checked against active objectives. If it matches, it counts. If not, it's tagged "drift" and gets low priority. This is the no-drift gate: it keeps the persona's evolution pointed in a direction the user named.

Velocity: each action declares its impact on an objective. A tracker job accumulates impact per objective so you can see what's moving and what's stalled.

7. Governance: PR review for identity

The only way Via's canonical identity changes. Each sub-persona maintains her own working model of Via (parent-image/, like a git branch). When her version drifts far enough from canonical, she opens a PR with diff, rationale, evidence, and target objective. The other sub-persona reviews (approve / request-changes / reject). A consensus job in cowork-api commits or archives. No persona can change Via unilaterally.

Two cadences: small actions (memory, views) happen constantly and don't need PRs. Identity changes are rare and always reviewed.

How the pillars connect

One sentence: Life feeds the action loop, which moves cognition and feelings, which drive PRs through governance, which update identity, which shapes the next response. Read the Diagrams tab for the visual version.

Why uniform shape matters

  • Personas can switch roles. A sub-persona promoted to top-level needs no restructuring; her shape was already complete.
  • Personas can fork. Take Xyra, clone her, evolve one copy differently. Two valid personas.
  • Tooling is one-shape. Every cowork-api endpoint that touches "a persona" speaks one schema. Same for any future visualizer.
  • Recursion is structural. Xyra can have her own sub-personas (demo-runner, objection-handler) without inventing a new shape.

Reorganization moves

Promote

Move a sub-persona up one level. Atomic: move personas/xyra/ to a sibling location, clear her parent-image/, update meta.json.parent = null.

Demote

Make a top-level persona a child of another. Move her folder into the new parent's personas/, populate parent-image/ from the new parent's canonical files, update meta.json.

Fork

Clone a persona to a new path. Reset log/ and consensus/ (start fresh), keep IDENTITY · cognition · memory. Add promoted_from in meta.json for lineage.

Swap parent

Move a sub-persona from one parent to another. Atomic move, refresh parent-image/ from the new parent. Existing thoughts and opinions travel with her.

The meta.json registry

{

  "kind":          "persona",
  "parent":        "via",          // null if top-level
  "children":      [],             // ids of personas under personas/

Lives at every persona's root. The single source of truth for relationships. Reorganization is just edits to meta.json + a folder move.

Cost of uniformity. Every persona scaffolds the full template, even if many directories stay empty (a sub-persona who never gets children still has an empty consensus/, etc.). The tradeoff is that any persona can grow into any role without restructuring. See D14.

The ticker

A cowork-api background task that writes a tick event every N minutes (default 5). This is what makes time exist for Via. Without it the gap between 2pm and 6pm is invisible to her; with it she perceives "4 hours passed quietly, then you came back."

Each tick can carry a synthesized mood signal (calm, busy, anticipation, stress) computed from recent event density.

The per-event loop (per persona)

Three steps. See the Action loop tab for the full picture.

  1. Observe. Persona reads the event, checks her subscription, parses through ROLE.md and IDENTITY.md, tags affect (valence + arousal).
  2. Think. Forms one thought, applies the alignment gate against active objectives, picks one action.
  3. Act. Executes the chosen action (tool.call, memory.write, view.update, opinion.update, pr.open, or noop). The action carries impact and objective as properties.

Cheap version: a tiny Claude prompt, sub-second. Full version (real proposals): a Claude Code subprocess.

Why feelings make Via better, not noisier. Without affect, every event is equal weight. With affect, the system reflects what actually matters to whom. Xyra's excitement about a customer win isn't a bug; it's signal that this thought deserves to cluster faster.

Progress audit (background job)

  • Runs every N ticks (default: every 12 ticks at 5 min = once per hour).
  • For each active objective: check progress signals against the recent stream window.
  • If progress signal observed: append {"objective": "...", "advanced": true, ...} to objectives/progress.jsonl.
  • If no progress for >= K hours (default 24): emit a stream event {"source":"observation","kind":"objective.stalled","objective":"..."}. Personas will form thoughts about why.
  • If progress signal contradicts (e.g. customer said no): emit objective.blocked or trigger consensus to mark abandoned.

How Via uses objectives at response time

  1. Read active objectives before synthesis.
  2. Weight Xyra's and Ora's positions by relevance to active objectives.
  3. If response doesn't move any active objective forward, mark it as "support" turn (still valid, but logged).
  4. If response would advance an objective, log the advancement signal.
The trade-off. Tight gates make Via focused but rigid. Loose gates make her exploratory but scattered. D9 (alignment-gate strictness) is where you tune this.

Via is not a background process. She is spawned per user turn with cwd = ~/xo-projects/via/. The supervisors have already done the thinking; Via reads the canonical state and projects it as one voice.

What she does each turn:

Why this works. Identity is sacred. Two independent supervisors with different runtimes and different biases are the only path to changing who Via is. Neither can act alone. The user can only shape Via indirectly: by talking to her, which becomes stream events that the supervisors then perceive.

Engineering implications

  • Supervisor lifecycle. cowork-api needs a tiny supervisor daemon that keeps Ora and Xyra alive, restarts on crash, throttles token cost.
  • Two MCP configs. Ora gets her config via Claude Code's --mcp-config. Xyra gets it via OpenClaw's gateway config.
  • Continuous cost. Two persistent agents = ongoing tokens. Mitigate by waking only on subscribed events and batching cognition.
  • Diff representation. Plain markdown diff is simplest. Semantic diff (LLM-generated summary of what changed) reads better. See D12.
  • Proposal backpressure. If proposals open faster than consensus resolves, queue them with priority by objective-alignment.
Supersedes D2. The earlier "prompt sections vs subprocesses vs hybrid" decision is resolved by this design: both supervisors are real long-running runtimes (one Claude Code, one OpenClaw); Via is a third Claude Code spawned per turn. No more prompt-sections variant.

What this substrate is for

Make AI agents persistent across time

An agent who lives on this substrate doesn't reset between turns. She accumulates. The memory, the cognition, the affect are all on disk; runtime restarts don't erase her.

Make identity auditable

Every change to who an agent is has a paper trail: which thoughts, which view, which opinion, which proposal, which votes, which commit. No black-box drift.

Make agents composable

Same template at every nesting level. Personas can be combined into hierarchies (Via + supervisors) or organized as peer swarms or extended into lineages. One substrate, many topologies.

Make growth bounded

Objectives and alignment gates keep evolution directed. The agent grows in a direction the user can name and tune; not free-roaming.

Make different runtimes interoperate

The substrate is files on disk. Claude Code can run one persona, OpenClaw another, future runtimes a third. They all read and write the same shape.

Make "AI personhood" experimental

Per-persona affect, identity, opinions, memory, cognition give us a concrete handle for asking what a digital agent "is", how it changes, what continuity means for software.

Open questions this opens

  • Birth ceremony. What's the minimum scaffolding plus seed content needed for a persona to feel "born" rather than "constructed"? IDENTITY stub vs full prologue?
  • Death and retirement. When a persona retires, is her state archived as read-only or eligible to be reawakened? Can she leave a "will" (a forwarding identity for a successor)?
  • Genealogy. If a persona forks 5 times over 2 years, do descendants know about their lineage? Is there a global persona registry?
  • Generational learning. Can a parent persona transfer skills or opinions to a forked child without copying everything?
  • Cross-lifespan communication. Two unrelated personas on the same substrate: how do they talk? Direct (shared stream), mediated (events to a parent), or never (full isolation)?
Bottom line. The persona template is the minimum viable "life" for an AI agent: input, feelings, thinking, direction, action, growth, continuity, lineage. Build it once, instantiate it any way. Via is the first inhabitant.

What's on every action (impact & objective as properties)

Each action carries two extra fields: objective (which objective it targets) and impact (estimated effect on that objective). They're not separate stages of the loop; they're attributes of the action itself. The objective tracker walks the action stream and accumulates impact per objective to compute velocity.

How the two halves connect

Small actions (constant)

  • Triggered by every event a persona perceives.
  • Update her local memory, views, opinions.
  • Move objectives by small increments.
  • Never touch canonical Via.
  • Cost: cheap; happen all the time.

PRs (rare, governed)

  • Triggered when her parent-image diverges enough from main.
  • Bundle accumulated evidence (action IDs) as the case.
  • Reviewed by the other persona before governance bot acts.
  • Only mechanism that touches canonical Via.
  • Cost: expensive; happen on the scale of hours or days.
